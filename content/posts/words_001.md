<p><span class="dropcap">I</span>s this the point where a socialist turned into an anarchist? Did this just happen? I never gave it much thought really. I guess that I thought Anarchy is just utopian, like all its critics do, thinking it is probably not going to work, which is besides the point, as no other system has proven to work, certainly not the current one.
I'll start at that moment, or the moment just before: I was running up a hill. A hill that I run almost daily. It is not an easy climb, but I am used to it, and my mind busily wanders and then.. I am at the top before I know it.
I was thinking, not sure what about. The voice in my head was judging and calling one opinion 'mature', and demanding therefore that opinion to be accepted, being close to 'objective' and 'correct' and 'true'. Yet surely there is no objective point of view and whatever idea depends perspective, on a point of view, as well as on context. What we call 'mature' represents a so called 'mature' point of view, and is not free from an interest in a specific outcome.</p>

{{< img src="images/words_001/color_inner_03.jpg" alt="stars are dust" caption="Stars are dust" class="center-image" >}}
<p>Individauls adopt ideas that support their stance, justify their existence and behaviour. People are born to the left and to the right, the ideas that appeal to them are those that confirm their position, so they can keep being themselves. Some might move away from their inherited disposition due to a personal story; this justifies in the same way why they adopt certain ideas and reject others.
Currently we are running our most important democratic institutions by the allowing the exclusive dominion of the most popular idea. Democracy has evolved into a system to govern, and a game to be won by those who write the rules. This combination forever erodes the ideas that are less popular, regardless of their validity, and so we ignore truth, wisdom, fairness, or any other virtue unless it happens to be popular.
This is not how it works in nature. It is not "the survival of the fittest", or whatever other evolutionary interpertation one might apply to describe it. In nature all the ideas live side by side, or in and out of eachother.
Which brings us to anarchism.</p>

{{< img src="images/words_001/color_inner_04.jpg" alt="coop" caption="coop" class="center-image" >}}
<p>More about biases; our pre dispostions and prejudices:</p>
<p>A few years ago, I read in parallel two books that were recommended to me. Both are about trends, and about where we come from and where we might be heading. One is called <a href="https://www.gapminder.org/" target="_blank">Factfulness</a> by the late Hans Rosling, and the other is <a href="https://www.cbc.ca/radio/ideas/the-2004-cbc-massey-lectures-a-short-history-of-progress-1.2946872" target="_blank">A short history of progress</a> by Ronald Wright. Wrights' book is describing a civilization that is responsible to its own destruction, for him progress is a trap, while Rosling is painting a rosy future, where our progress banishes poverty and promotes longevity. Wright's book is endorsed by environmentalists, and Rosling's book endorsed by capitalists. Interesting that both books rely on facts, and so the bias rises from the choice of facts.</p>

<p>Finally I'd like to look at physics; I for a while was fascinated with the forth dimension, well with any number of dimensions, and in my reading I came across some visual  explanation that opened up a window for me. It was about our blind spots. We, who live in 3 dimensions, are blind to events that happen in a dimension that we don't have sensory awareness of. That is to say that for an event that happens in another dimension we can measure the possible effect in our plane, but we can't explain what happened as we are lacking data.</p>
<p>Phycists are building worlds in that realm, since they had to invent a math that explains things they measure but can't be explained with the data gathered, something is missing. oh... and the visual explanation I came across was quite simple, it was showing a graph in two dimensions that shows some tendency, let's say the graph was representing general growth on the y axis in relation with the increased value of x. Now looking in those two dimensions you can tell a story, but if you expand the graph to include a third dimensions, a whole new story can appear, a boring straight graph, can have a sharp change in the z axis that is not at all noticeable looking only at the x and y. Our understanding of the cause and possible affects of a change in the values of the x and y can be very different once we include the extra axis. The context, as I said was extra dimensions in the study of physics, how our understanding of natural phenomenon would change if other dimenesions were, so to say, visible to us. In some cases the affect might be dramatic, but the cause is invisible, in other cases there might be a small negligeble effect, but we might just have missed something huge.</p>


{{< img src="images/words_001/color_inner_05.jpg" alt="ploughed field" caption="Ploughed field" class="center-image" >}}


<p>So why am I mentioning the extra dimensions? well, I find that it is an alegory to our blindness to other points of view, and the truth of those that we don't hold. In a world where ideas compete to become the governing idea, and in a system where democracy is interperted to be the rule of the most popular idea, rather than, a perhaps more mature interpertation that takes in account that all ideas are born of a pre disposition. Isn't that more like nature? instead of utilizing the idea of nature to promote a social concept from Darwinism to Liberterianism, or speaking about selfish as natural ( as they do when a leader is proven to be corrupt ), I suggest to learn from nature. Nature is multi faceted, nature desregards personal will, nature does not have lobbies, and centres of power, nature has no preferences, it favours nothing. We are the only species that can decide to ignore nature, or to work against it.</p>

<p>The simple mantra proposed in this humble letter, is "Things you see from here, you don't see from there". I find it explains a lot about my neighbours, my fellow workmates, my fellow countrymen and women, ones family..</p>
