<p><span class="dropcap">T</span>here is a story I was telling, one I retold many times yet I haven't told it for a while. Now, getting back to telling the story I've noticed some gaps that I did not know how to fill, and I was getting this odd feeling: `perhaps this never happened?`. I then stopped and reminded myself that I don't make up such stories, that I have no reason to, and that I find enough interest and weirdness in reality not to come up with lies. Well, here is the story:
</p>
<p>I was in a city, I don't remember which city or in which country. I was in a part of the city that I was not familiar with. I ended up in that part and my glasses' frame broke, and so I found an optician shop. I remember it more like a hardware shop. There was an old man behind a counter. The counter that I remember had shutters and since I don't remember entering through a door, that counter was on the street. I am not sure what kind of an optician has a setup like that.. My story fails in the introduction and I have no memory to help it up. I handed the old man my glasses to fix and told him that I will just wait, as I have no replacement. He said `come back in an hour`, so I carefuly made my way across the road to a park and sat on a bench. I can't see a thing without my glasses and I have no memory of how I passed that hour, imagination and memory here intertwine. When the hour was up I went over to the counter, the old man was handling something. He looked at me and said `Hello there, how can I help you?`, I told him I left him my glasses to fix an hour ago and he went to a back room for a while. He didn't appear to recognise me. When he finally came back, he looked at me with that same look, as if my presence distracted him from some task, and said `Hello there, how can I help you?`. I had no choice but to repeat that I left my glasses with him and he was just fetching them, I then pointed at what he was holding, as it looked familiar in that blur. Those were my glasses he was holding.
</p>
{{< img src="images/penart_003/10.jpg" alt="Masked" caption="Masked" class="center-image" >}}
<p>This time round telling the story caused me great embarrassment. As I was telling it I saw it made no sense, and I had to stop. I remembered how my dad told me a few times that often it happens to him. A story that was told many times and is no longer his. Remembering a retelling and not the memory.</p>

{{< img src="images/penart_003/18.jpg" alt="Rocket" caption="Rocket" class="center-image" >}}
<p> A similar category of un-truths I experienced as a child. Stories of others that appealed to me and I willed them mine. My brother was one such hero, the stories of his wars with the greater neighborhood gangs, the stories of a braver, more solid generation that felt much more valid than our own, five years younger, too late, and so unprepared.</p>

{{< img src="images/penart_003/12.jpg" alt="Do I know you?" caption="Do I know you?" class="center-image" >}}
<p>The images here are from a notebook I started on June 2025 and finished in July 2026, it was hard going. Airports, hospital, death. Memories of places I haven't visited for 25 years, and even when I did visit I never really stopped.

The method of drawing is a bit hardcore. I start not knowing where it is going. I learned not to mind the embarrassment, not to mind people interpretations, their remarks about my subconscious. I feel that the subconscious is common it is not mine.</p>
