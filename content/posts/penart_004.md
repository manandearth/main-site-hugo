<p><span class="dropcap">F</span>eeling that I reached my limit with ball point, or at least for now, I am now fully submerged in black permanent. Three fountain pens by nib size and a Kuretake brush pen, all feeding from the one ink pot. The limit I meet regularly, sometime because I lack the instructions and sometimes for quite the opposite reason, instruction can bring me to the edge: A few years ago I became fascinated with cornets. It was before Christmas in Durham, staying with my inlaws. The jazz records my father in law played, the street brass bands, I was swimming in horns. The neighbour over a cup of tea, hearing me going on, got up and got two cases out of the cabinet under the stairs, one with a cornet and one with a trumpet. I started practicing right away, by the time we were back in Spain I was going up and down scales and improvising little tunes. Playing a cornet is not like a piano or a guitar, your neighbours know you play the cornet, your neighbours neighbours also, so one day I got tipped that the towns band leader is taking students and I went over to see him. He must have been under forty and his passion was his trumpet, he kept it super clean. He prepared a little printout for me to take with a diagram and the exercises I am meant to practice at home. When I blew my first note, he corrected me "not like this" and he blew one long note. The exercise was playing long notes and short notes and his instruction was to give this torture 15 minutes a day. I called him the next day to call it off. Ignorance is bliss.</p>
{{< img src="images/penart_004/10.jpg" alt="sou'wester" caption="sou'wester" class="center-image" >}}
<p>A few months after this episode I was brought a broken old clarinet to repair, at the time I was building Spanish guitars in a little workshop, but I wouldn't shy from any job. I played around with it and cleaned it up and try to figure out what was missing. I ordered a set of pads, some cork strips, and reeds and then on a second thought, I ordered a new clarinet as reference. The Clarinet I thought is a melodic and monophonic, yet without that wild embouchure threshold that the cornet propose. The books followed, and the playing infront of the mirror, and the fun practice of jazz melodies and hasidic Klezmer. Then one day I looked for tutorials on Youtube and in three minutes I learned that my technic is wrong and there is no escape from the exact same drills the trumpet teacher printed for me.</p>
